# Abridgment selection rubric

This rubric proposes how to select sections for a reader-interest abridgment;
it records no selection or translation. Apply it only after the Lindsay Latin
inventory is imported and collated. Preserve source references and disclose
omissions so the abridgment is never mistaken for the complete work.

## Reader-interest screen

Prioritize passages that are vivid, intelligible in context, and useful to a
general reader. Look first for material on:

- language, words, names, and Isidore's explanations of them;
- animals and their observed or reported traits;
- medicine, the body, health, and illness;
- the world, geography, sky, seasons, and weather;
- materials, tools, buildings, and craft;
- daily life, food, work, learning, customs, and public institutions.

These are discovery lenses, not claims that a passage exists or is reliable.
Do not invent cross-references or modern sources. Label ancient reports as
Isidore's claims; add a modern reference only after checking the source.

## Selection and sequence

Score each numbered section 0–2 for reader interest, self-contained
readability, and value as a representative example of the work. Record a short
reason and source evidence for every score. Favor self-contained selections
that preserve the complete section. A fragment may still be included when its
reader interest warrants it: mark the gap plainly, identify the omitted source
span, and do not supply speculative completion. Record any cuts and rationale.

Use the scores to inform editorial judgment, not as an automatic inclusion
threshold. Balance topics and books, retain distinctive examples, and avoid
repetition. Keep manageable sections and chapter groupings where practical.
Defer textually difficult or context-dependent passages for editorial review;
never silently repair them or hide uncertainty. Publish a
contents list and a brief selection note describing scope and exclusions.

## Record per candidate

Record `ref`, exact source-file/hash, section text or excerpt locator, topic
tags, three scores, rationale, context needed, textual issues, decision
(`include`, `defer`, `exclude`), decision date, and editor. Revisit decisions
when the collated Latin changes. A decision is not a coverage claim: retain
the full-work manifest and report abridgment coverage separately.
