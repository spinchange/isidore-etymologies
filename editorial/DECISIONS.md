# Editorial decisions

## 2026-11-01: pilot approved

The editor approved the pilot in full and described the reading as enjoyable
and effortless. Keep its contemporary prose, short Latin terms within the
English, concise selection notes, and 6x9 page design as the working standard.
This approval establishes the register and design; it does not certify future
translations or replace their source checks.

The first release remains an explicitly abridged edition. A complete edition
is a welcome longer-term possibility, not an obligation to translate all twenty
books for this release. Keep sources, references, and accepted text reusable.

The trivium is a prominent focus: grammar, rhetoric, and dialectic. The editor
specifically requested rhetoric and 'trivium type stuff.' The next batch adds
speaking, persuasion, argument, and history as a branch of grammar.

## Working extent and shape (editorial planning proposal)

Aim first at a coherent single-volume reader, provisionally about 25,000-35,000
translated words plus concise notes. This is a planning range, not an approved
page count or a reason to pad the selection. Final extent follows actual text
and typesetting. Topic balance and readability matter more than equal quotas
for the twenty source books.

Proposed thematic parts:

1. Words, learning, and memory: grammar, origins of words, writing, history.
2. Speaking and thinking: rhetoric, persuasion, figures, dialectic, definition.
3. Living things: selected bodies, animals, and plants.
4. Earth and sky: selected weather, geography, waters, and materials.
5. The made and everyday world: tools, buildings, clothing, food, and measures.

The selection should preserve Isidore's intellectual and religious setting
where it shapes a passage. Reader interest includes unfamiliar explanations
and surprising claims, not only material that sounds modern. Prefer complete
units; allow worthwhile fragments with explicit gaps and no invented repairs.
