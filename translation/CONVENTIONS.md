# Translation conventions — initial proposals

The following establishes a starting register. Refine it after the pilot;
do not treat it as evidence of decisions already tested on the whole work.

- Clear contemporary English prose; retain Isidore's organization and voice.
- Align by numbered Latin section, with paragraph breaks where useful.
- Preserve etymological reasoning even where modern linguistics rejects it.
  Keep the relevant Latin word visible when English would erase the wordplay,
  and explain the connection in a note when necessary.
- Distinguish the author's claim from the editor's correction or explanation.
- Preserve quotations and their role in the argument; verify attributions.
- Retain Greek, Hebrew, abbreviations, technical vocabulary, and textual cruces
  accurately. Establish transliteration policy from actual pilot examples.
- Use familiar English names when unambiguous, recording consistent choices.
- Notes cite book.chapter.section. Public references must survive changes in
  pagination and must be shared by the website and print edition.
- No meter, syllable band, or verse-line alignment requirement applies here.
