# Translation conventions - abridged edition

The editor approved the initial pilot register on 2026-11-01. Carry it into
new selections, with separate fidelity review for each. These decisions have
not been tested on the whole work.

- Clear contemporary English prose; retain Isidore's organization and voice.
- Align by numbered Latin section, with paragraph breaks where useful.
- Preserve etymological reasoning even where modern linguistics rejects it.
  Keep the relevant Latin word visible when English would erase the wordplay,
  and explain the connection in a note when necessary.
- Distinguish the author's claim from the editor's correction or explanation.
- Preserve quotations and their role in the argument; verify attributions.
- Retain Greek, Hebrew, abbreviations, technical vocabulary, and textual cruces
  accurately. Establish transliteration policy from actual pilot examples.
- Use familiar English names when unambiguous, recording consistent choices.
- Notes cite book.chapter.section. Public references must survive changes in
  pagination and must be shared by the website and print edition.
- No meter, syllable band, or verse-line alignment requirement applies here.

## Abridgment and Latin on the page

- Select complete numbered sections or a complete chapter when possible.
  State exactly what is included; a selected chapter opening is not a complete
  chapter. Thematic headings are ours, not translations of ancient headings.
- Prefer reader interest over sheer ease. Simple, self-contained material is
  useful for production; curious fragments remain eligible with labeled gaps.
- English carries the argument; short italic Latin terms expose the etymology.
  Keep both sides of a Latin sound/spelling comparison, such as lucus/luceat.
  Do not reproduce full facing Latin by default in this print design.
- Give meanings alongside unfamiliar forms. Use inflected source forms where
  helpful; a transparent dictionary headword can be used, but the term record
  must identify its exact source form. Keep Greek when the source uses Greek.
- Notes explain what the main text cannot show economically. Avoid a note
  merely repeating the paragraph. Distinguish original reports from present
  editorial explanation; do not silently modernize ancient claims.
- Square brackets in the Latin reproduce Lindsay's editorial brackets, not
  abridgment omissions. Translate the content; explain important uncertainty
  in a note. Use a labeled omission marker only for an actual editorial cut.
- 'Slaves' translates servi; the hive's reges remain 'kings'. Do not make
  historical institutions or ancient natural history more comfortable by
  silently changing the words.

Displayed term metadata identifies Latin or Greek actually shown in English,
never an English translation to be italicized. Map an inflected source form to
a headword explicitly. Whole-form, case-sensitive checks prevent accidental
substring matches; lead review still checks meaning.
